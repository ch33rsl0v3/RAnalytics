## How to read this

- `heat` is players per million lifetime visits, built on the median CCU of the last day rather than the live reading. High means traffic is arriving now rather than accumulated, which is the launching signal.
- Ratios between two cumulative numbers (`fav/1k`) are steady. Anything with live CCU on top swings with the hour, which is why heat and `visits per player` are smoothed and why a game needs several readings before it appears on a board at all.
- `fav/1k` is favorites per thousand visits. It does not predict take-off: in the backtest games under 3 took off more often than the rest, because players the algorithm sends rarely favourite anything.
- Median is used everywhere instead of mean so one giant game cannot carry a term.
- A term only appears if several different developers use it and no single game supplies most of its players. That keeps one hit title from making its own vocabulary look like a trend.
- `visits per player` is lifetime visits divided by players online now. Low means the audience arrived recently, which is the launching signal; an old hit has millions of visits behind each current player.
- `past peak` is the share of a term's games already below their own best day. A term can top the trending list while most of its games are on the way down - that is how a dead theme looks busy.
- `vs market` is the game's change divided by the whole platform's change over the same stretch. Roblox runs far quieter on a weekday afternoon than on a Sunday evening, so a raw percentage compares calendars; 1.0 here means the game moved exactly with everything else, 2.0 means it doubled relative to the market.
- `copies` (Still unclaimed) is how many other games share this one's title shape. Zero means the shape is still unspoken for; the clone burst for a hit tends to land about a month after it.
- `theme` (Taking off, radar) is how far other developers have got: no copies yet / copies (games from other developers created after it that share its title shape or a word nobody used before it) / follows an older hit with the same title shape, so the theme is not new. Only games the tracker has seen count.
- `devs` is how many distinct developers use the term. Low means the term may belong to one studio rather than the market.

## Known limits of this data

- Retention, icon CTR, player age and player country are NOT available for anyone else's games. Creator Dashboard only, own games only.
- Session length is a Little's Law estimate and runs low, because rejoins count as fresh visits.
- Revenue is not public anywhere. Only pass prices are.
- Creation dates lie on reskins and renames.
- The corpus comes from Discover, which promotes games that already won, so brand-new releases are under-represented.
